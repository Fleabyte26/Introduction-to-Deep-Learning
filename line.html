A camera on the line: from video to one row per fruit

Fruit moves down a belt, one piece a second. A camera films it at 30 frames a second. Half an hour later an arm at the end of the line has to push the bad ones into a bin. Everything this series has covered is in the gap between those two moments. This page runs the whole thing with drawn avocados and a small real network grading them live in your browser, and follows one fruit from the first frame to its row in a table.

What is real here, and what is not

  

    
Real. The small network: 12,461 weights, three convolution layers, trained on 6,000 drawn fruit and right on 97.0% of 2,000 it had not seen. It runs in this page on every fruit that passes. Every accuracy figure is a measured result. The sixteen written inspections are what Claude wrote when shown those sixteen stills, recorded before the true conditions were looked at.

    
Not real. The fruit are drawings, which are far easier than photographs: real skin, real lighting and real dirt would cost both models accuracy. The tracking is simulated, since the page knows where each fruit is. A real line needs a detector to find the fruit in each frame. The second look is not run live from this page; the request shown is the format a call to the Messages API would use, and nothing is sent. Lengths are read from the drawing's known scale.

    
The fragile link. A reference number made from a time stamp only finds the right fruit at the arm if the fruit keep their order for the whole 30 minutes. If they roll, bunch up or pass through a buffer, the number points at the wrong fruit. Lines solve this with cups or pockets on the belt, or a second camera at the arm.

    
The training and measurement code is in the repository under experiments. This page puts to work the ideas from the earlier episodes: convolutional networks, confusion matrices, how much data a network needs, and large pretrained models.
