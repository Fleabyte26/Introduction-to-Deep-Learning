Why the survivors are scaled up

Each unit is kept with probability 1 − p. If the survivors kept their plain value, the next layer would receive less signal on average during training than at prediction time, when nothing is dropped. Dividing by 1 − p fixes that: on average, a unit sends p × 0 + (1 − p) × h / (1 − p) = h, exactly what it sends with dropout off. The blue bars show that average building up.

From Section 5.6 of Dive into Deep Learning. The book's network uses p = 0.5 after both hidden layers, and suggests a lower probability closer to the input. Dropout is only applied during training. The idea behind it is that a network should not rely on any single unit being present, so the units cannot become dependent on one particular partner.
