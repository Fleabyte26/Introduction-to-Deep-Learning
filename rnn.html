Recurrent neural networks: a network with a memory

A convolutional network looks at a whole picture at once. A sentence does not arrive that way. It arrives one piece at a time, and what a piece means depends on what came before. A recurrent network reads one character, updates a small memory, and carries that memory to the next character. Below is a real one. It has 24 numbers of memory and was trained on two sentences: the cat sat on the mat. and the dog sat on the log. Step through a sentence and watch what it remembers.

One set of weights, used at every step

Drawn out along the sentence, a recurrent network looks like a long chain. It is not. It is one small network, used again at every character. A longer sentence means more steps, not more weights.

How good is a guess: perplexity

Perplexity answers one question: on average, how many characters is the network still choosing between? Pure guessing among 14 characters gives 14. Always certain and always right gives 1.

Backpropagation through time: why memory fades

To learn that m depends on cat, the error at the m has to travel backwards through every step in between, and each step multiplies it. Multiply by something below 1 many times and it fades to nothing. Above 1 and it explodes. The dark line is measured from the trained network above. The coloured line is the simple rule, a single number multiplied by itself. Drag the slider.

Two standard repairs. Gradient clipping: if the signal grows past a limit, scale it back down, which stops explosions. Truncation: only send the error back a fixed number of steps, which keeps training affordable. Neither one fixes fading. That takes a different kind of memory cell, the LSTM, which is the next page.
