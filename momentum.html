Momentum: a heavy ball that remembers

This valley is gentle along x₁ and steep along x₂. Plain gradient descent has to choose: a small step that crawls along the floor, or a big step that bounces off the walls. Momentum averages recent gradients, so the bouncing cancels out and the steady push along the floor adds up. Try the four presets from the book.

What "remembering" means

How much each past gradient still counts in the current velocity

From Section 12.6 of Dive into Deep Learning. The function, the starting point (−5, −2) and the four presets are the book's. With η = 0.6 and β = 0.5 the book reports x₁ = 0.007188 and x₂ = 0.002553 after 20 steps, and this page gives the same numbers. The velocity is a "leaky average": v ← βv + gradient, then x ← x − ηv. A gradient from k steps ago still counts with weight βᵏ, and the weights add up to 1 / (1 − β).

Why it rescues the steep direction: in x₂ the gradients flip sign on every bounce, so in the average they mostly cancel. In x₁ they all point the same way, so they add. On a bowl like this, plain gradient descent is stable only if η times the curvature is below 2; momentum raises that limit to 2(1 + β).
