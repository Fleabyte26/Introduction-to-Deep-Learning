The transformer: attention, plus order, stacked

Attention on its own has a blind spot: it has no idea what order the words came in. The transformer fixes that with one addition, wraps attention in a block that can be stacked many layers deep, and removes the recurrent network completely. This page runs two small real transformers in your browser, one with word order and one without, and shows what each part of the block is for.

How order gets in

Every position gets its own fixed pattern of 16 numbers, made from sine and cosine waves of different speeds. That pattern is simply added to the word's vector. Nothing else in the network changes. The same word in a different place now arrives as a slightly different vector.

One block, stacked

A transformer is the same block repeated. Inside: attention, then a small ordinary network applied to each word. Around each of the two there is a skip connection that adds the input back, and a normalisation step. The skip connection is the same idea as the residual connection from the convolution episodes. Encoder, decoder, and who may look where

The original transformer has two stacks. An encoder reads the whole input. A decoder writes the output one word at a time. The only difference inside the block is a rule about which words each word is allowed to look at.

What this page left out

Sections 11.8 and 11.9 of the book continue from here: transformers for images, and pretraining. They are the next two episodes.

Details that vary. The two networks in the first panel use the 2017 layout. Many models learn the position patterns instead of using fixed waves, or build position into the attention step itself. The depth experiment uses one fixed learning rate; with a gentle warm-up, as in the paper, the 2017 layout trains deeper than it does here.

The two networks in the first panel are real, each with 2,536 weights: one block, two heads, vectors of 16 numbers. They were trained on sentences made from 8 animals and 4 verbs. The one with positions was tested on 88 sentences it never saw in training. The one without positions was trained on every one of the 448 possible sentences and still gets exactly half of them right. The depth results are real training runs of the same design. The training code is in the repository under experiments. Goes with section 11.7 of Dive into Deep Learning.
