Stochastic gradient descent: a noisy walk downhill

Plain gradient descent looks at every example before each step. Stochastic gradient descent looks at one, so each step is cheap but the direction is only roughly right. The fix for the wobble is to shrink the learning rate as training goes on. Pick a schedule and press Play.

From Section 12.4 of Dive into Deep Learning. The first panel is the book's experiment: start at (−5, −2), base learning rate 0.1, and random noise with standard deviation 1 added to each gradient to imitate picking a single example. The three schedules are the book's: constant, exponential (0.1 × e^(−0.1t)) and polynomial (0.1 × (1 + 0.1t)^(−0.5)). The book runs 50 steps for the constant and polynomial schedules; this page runs 100.

The second panel is the book's point about sampling. Picking n examples at random with replacement reaches any given example with probability 1 − (1 − 1/n)ⁿ, which is about 63%. That is why training code shuffles the data and walks through it once per epoch instead.
