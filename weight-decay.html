Weight decay: a leash on the weights

This model has 200 inputs but only 20 training examples, so it can memorize them. Weight decay adds a penalty for large weights, which shrinks every weight a little on each update. Press Play with λ = 0, then try λ = 3.

The setup is the experiment in Section 3.7 of Dive into Deep Learning: y = 0.05 + the sum of 0.01·xᵢ, plus a little noise, with 200 inputs, 20 training examples, 100 validation examples, minibatches of 5 and learning rate 0.01. After 10 epochs the book reports ‖w‖² / 2 of about 0.0099 without decay and 0.0017 with λ = 3. This page trains the same model live in your browser and lands close to both. It keeps going to epoch 30 so the curves have time to separate.

Why it works: only 20 directions in the 200-dimensional weight space are pinned down by the data. Without decay, the weights in the other 180 directions stay wherever they started, which is random. Decay pulls those toward zero, and zero is a better guess than random.
