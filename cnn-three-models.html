Three CNNs, five epochs: why the plain one won

Three versions of one small convolutional network, each trained for five epochs on CIFAR-10: 60,000 small colour pictures in ten classes. The first is plain. The second trains on augmented pictures. The third adds batch normalization and dropout. The two with the improvements lost, by 12 and 16 points. Every number on this page comes from that one real run. Press Play and watch the race, then see why the result is honest, and why it would not last.

The plain network, left running by accident

Run a training cell twice in a notebook and it does not start again. It carries on. The plain network’s cell was run three times in a row, by mistake, so the same network trained for 15 epochs instead of 5. (This was an earlier run, on 36,000 training pictures, so its first five epochs differ slightly from the race above.) That mistake recorded the part of the story the five-epoch race cannot show.

The final test, and what it does not say

Accuracy on 10,000 test pictures the networks never saw
Training accuracy minus validation accuracy at epoch 5, in points. Above zero means memorising has begun
The plain network is ahead, and that is a fair result for five epochs. But look at the second chart. The plain network already scores 2.4 points better on its own training pictures than on new ones: memorising has begun, and the 15-epoch run above shows where that leads. The augmented network scores no better on its training pictures than on new ones, and the dropout network scores 11.5 points worse. Neither has started to memorise at all. Augmentation and dropout make each step of learning harder, on purpose. Five epochs measures that cost and none of the benefit. A fair comparison would train all three until validation stops improving.

What the first layer learned

The first eight of the plain network’s 32 first-layer filters. Each is 3 pixels by 3 pixels, drawn in the colours it responds to. Click one.

Textbooks show crisp edge and stripe detectors. Those come from much larger first-layer filters trained for far longer. At 3 by 3 pixels after five epochs, most filters are a soft colour contrast: light on one side and dark on the other, or one tint against another. That is still an edge detector, just a very small one.

Concepts from chapters 7, 8 and 14 of Dive into Deep Learning: convolutions and pooling, batch normalization, dropout, and image augmentation. The numbers are from one training run on CIFAR-10 (Krizhevsky, 2009): 30,000 training pictures, 10,000 validation, 10,000 test, batch size 64, Adam. A different random start would move each number by a point or two.
